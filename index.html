What is Sharia Law?

Sharia is the moral and legal framework of Islam, derived from the Quran and the teachings of Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ). It governs religious rituals, daily life, ethics, business, family, and justice.

At its heart, Sharia aims to promote justice, compassion, and human welfare (maslaha). It is interpreted by qualified scholars through ijtihad applied to changing contexts.

Core Characteristics

✨ Divine Origin

Based on revelation (Quran) and Prophetic example (Sunnah)

🌍 Comprehensive

Covers all aspects of human life — spiritual, social, economic, political

🎯 Purposive

Aimed at achieving human welfare (maslaha) and preventing harm

🌱 Flexible

Contains mechanisms for adaptation to changing times and circumstances

Maqasid al-Sharia (Higher Objectives)

The ultimate goals of Sharia are to preserve five essential human interests:

1

Faith (Din)

Freedom of religious practice, preservation of religious values, protection of places of worship.

2

Life (Nafs)

Sanctity of life, strong burden of proof in capital cases, healthcare and medical ethics.

3

Intellect (Aql)

Preservation of mental faculties, prohibition of intoxicants, pursuit of knowledge.

4

Lineage (Nasl)

Family structure, marriage as legal framework, rights of children.

5

Property (Mal)

Safeguarding wealth, prohibition of theft & fraud, fair contracts, inheritance, zakat.

Primary & Secondary Sources

📖 Quran

Exact words of God. 114 surahs. ~500 legal verses form the foundation.

🕊️ Sunnah

Prophet's sayings, actions, and approvals. Clarifies Quranic injunctions.

🤝 Ijma

Consensus of qualified scholars. Used for new challenges.

🧠 Qiyas

Analogical reasoning — applying existing laws to new circumstances.

Madhhabs (Schools of Thought)

🟢 Hanafi

Imam Abu Hanifa (d.767). Most widely followed. Emphasizes qiyas. Prevalent in Turkey, Central/South Asia.

🟡 Maliki

Imam Malik (d.795). North Africa. Primacy of Medinan practice and public interest.

🔵 Shafi'i

Imam al-Shafi'i (d.820). Egypt, SE Asia. Systematized usul al-fiqh.

🔴 Hanbali

Imam Ahmad (d.855). Saudi Arabia, Qatar. Most literal. Strict adherence to hadith.

🟣 Ja'fari

Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq (d.765). Main Shia school. Iran, Iraq, Lebanon.

Quranic Foundations

5:48

"Judge between them by what Allah has revealed." — Foundation of divine law.

4:59

"Obey Allah and obey the Messenger…" — Establishes sources & hierarchy.

2:256

"No compulsion in religion." — Freedom of religious choice.

4:135

"Be standing firm in justice… even against yourselves." — Justice as core.

17:34

"Fulfill every commitment." — Foundation of contract law.

2:179

"In legal retribution is life." — Proportional justice.

Hadith Foundations

Intentions

"Actions are judged by intentions." (Bukhari & Muslim) — Fundamental maxim.

No Harm

"No harm nor reciprocating harm." (Ibn Majah) — Basis for tort law.

Burden of Proof

"Proof on claimant, oath on denier." (Tirmidhi)

Intoxicants

"Every intoxicant is forbidden." (Muslim) — Demonstrates qiyas.

Reconciliation

"Reconciliation is permissible among Muslims." (Abu Dawud)

Qawa'id Fiqhiyyah (Legal Maxims)

الأمور بمقاصدها

"Acts are judged by intentions." — Validity depends on intention.

اليقين لا يزول بالشك

"Certainty is not removed by doubt."

المشقة تجلب التيسير

"Hardship begets facility." — Concessions during difficulty.

الضرر يزال

"Harm must be eliminated."

العادة محكمة

"Custom is authoritative." — Local customs valid when not contradicting text.
